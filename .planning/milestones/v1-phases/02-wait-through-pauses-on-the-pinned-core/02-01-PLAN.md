Move the adapter onto the wait-through-pauses core and prove, end to end on the real core, that a
paused F.A.S.T. step stays open and ends with the run's real outcome.

Leads with the phase tracer: one Transfer, wired from the gRPC wire through base_server, the
re-pinned `FastService.run_transfer` and its poll loop, to an instrument pause recorded in
`run_state`, an operator Resume through the Phase 1 `ExecuteCommand` override, and SUCCESS (D-15).

commit, so the gate stays green. Then RunProtocol gets the same wiring with operator- and
instrument-pause slices (D-04). Plan 02-02 then teaches the gate to reject a core without the
capability or the state gates (D-03) and updates the stale docstrings (D-05).

Purpose: the real-core threading test is the phase's architectural risk (research Pattern 3: an
Event-gated get_status deadlocks against ResumeMarker). Proving it first means a dead end shows up
after one task, not after the abort and timeout work is built on it.
Output: the new pin, the two-kwarg wiring in Transfer and RunProtocol, the real-core test fixture
(`_Stepper`, `_stub_transport`, `_real_core`, `_RealCoreGrpcTestCase`) and `TestPauseThroughRealCore`.
